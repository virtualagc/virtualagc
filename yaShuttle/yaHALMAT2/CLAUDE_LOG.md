(Correction to this bullet's original wording: I'd said HELLO's `WRITE(PRINTER)` statement -- ~209.6us/execution real, IOINIT+COUTP+IOINIT+CATV -- was a "~700x undercount" against "~297ns currently modeled." That's a unit error: 1 tick at the *old* 16800-ticks/sec rate is 1e6/16800 ~= 59.52 MICROseconds, not nanoseconds, so 5 non-zero-cost opcodes x 1 tick was actually ~297.6us modeled -- an OVER-count of ~42% for that specific statement, not a 700x undercount. The real picture, confirmed once Phase 1 below was implemented and validated: pure-compute opcodes were being substantially OVER-priced by the old flat 59.52us/tick charge (real local costs mostly run 0-30us), while I/O/RTL-heavy statements were UNDER-priced (RTL call costs of 20-200+us/call weren't modeled via any opcode at all) -- a mixed picture, not a uniform direction, which is exactly what the pre-existing per-fixture spread (HELLO under-corrected, scalar_arith over-corrected) already implied.)

- User asked to chase down the INVERSE/TRANSPOSE/TRACE gap instead of leaving it. Root-caused (the "not root-caused" note above was wrong): yaGPC2's own H= word indices are numbered against **optmat.bin** (the optimizer-expanded stream it actually executes), not halmat.bin -- confirmed by direct disassembly, `--disasm halmat.bin` was simply missing whole index ranges (e.g. #139 didn't exist) that `--disasm optmat.bin` had.
