### [2026-08-07] Target: [mafgenComparison.md]
- The SPSPSP family is characterised. All 26 unexplained halfwords across G9 and S2 ($0SPSPSP, A4/A5/A6SPSPSP, $0VG9OPS, $0SM2OPS) share one signature: the HIGH byte is identical in every case and only the low byte differs, by +0x6, +0xC, +0x14, +0x18 or +0x1C — always positive, always even. The halfword is an instruction whose opcode and register are unchanged and whose DISPLACEMENT grew, so it is a base-register-relative access, not an address. That is why there is no RLD at the site and why owner() can never see it: there is nothing to relocate.
- Corroborated at S2 A6SPSPSP 0x10E18, where the adjacent halfword DOES carry an RLD into #PCSPCLB (revised BN→BO) and is already excused as revision drift. Positive even deltas are the insertion signature: the build's variables sit further from the base than ours.
- The rigorous route to attributing these is HALSTAT's own per-variable offset table for the revised COMPOOL against ours — the same evidence already used for CSPCLB (dump at offsets 67/86/88 where we place the same variables at 62/80/82). If a variable we place at displacement 0x86 is at 0x92 in HALSTAT, the difference is confirmed rather than inferred. Not yet done; it is the correct next step, not a blocker.
- Correction to the earlier entry: the claim that a recovered address overlapping a scraped CSECT must be wrong is FALSE. Nesting is the norm — a COMPOOL whose storage is owned by an assembly module lives inside that module's CSECT, which is dass-syms.py's entire premise. A guard on that basis rejected #PCDHMMU, the documented type case, and was withdrawn. What was real is that owner() picked the outermost of several containing spans; it now takes the narrowest (commit ae3dee531). #PCVTTCS at 0xBE1A-0xD718 across #PCSAPDT genuinely does conflict, but that is a partial overlap, not nesting, and it is not evidence that the recovery is wrong.
- HALSFC is a plain Python program invoked by path, not a pip install, so it CAN be copied and instrumented while a sweep runs. Only lnk101 and fcmcmp are genuinely blocked by an in-flight sweep. The PASS1 via-double literal call site is therefore not blocked after all.
